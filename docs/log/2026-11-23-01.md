## Tried and dropped

- **The depth lead (D50).** Dropped on the evidence: at M/V = 1.01 m Response's stages
  are those of 1 m; at 0.99 m its step is half as long (M/V under the depth, a rule r2c
  has), one regular stage falls where the top is still cracked (50.0 kN, r2c 49.9), and
  no stage is put in between there either. So do not look again for rounding of M/V
  against the depth. What is left open: no stage put in at 0.99, 1 and 1.01 m, all of
  them at 2 m.
- **Transverse bond with prestrained stirrups, before the binary was read.** Applying
  r2c's bond rule to Response's own stirrup stress chart gave 355 % at the peak, so the
  rule was wrong for this case, not r2c's state. Arithmetic on the chart values did not
  find the rule; the routines did. Do not try to fit that chart at the bottom end of the
  stirrups stage by stage: Response's own values there go 0.13, 18.6, 37.2, 1.4,
  37.3 MPa at successive stages, with which thin-layer nodes fall under its test for no
  shear strain.
- **Reading zero stirrup stress off a chart as "a node with no shear strain"** in
  `tools/bond.py`: right only where the stirrups are prestrained. On plain Newbeam an
  uncracked node also shows no stress, and treating it so gave differences up to 79 %
  that are the tool's, not the rule's. The tool now does it only with a prestrain.
- **Putting the no-shear-strain rule into the force evaluation** was not tried: only the
  reported state has it (Decisions).

  stage reports and not to the force evaluation. Why: the charts are where it shows;
  the stirrups carry no part of N or M; and a change in the force evaluation of every
  shear case would have to be weighed against the no-regression bound for no check it
  could bring inside. The test uses r2c's solved transverse strain where the program
  uses the node's last one; the difference in the limit is under 1 %.

  the differences of the "before" run, as for the earlier ones.
