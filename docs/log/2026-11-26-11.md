# 2026-11-26-11: increment 7, speed joins the threshold: r2x timed, r2c with shear several times faster with every record unchanged; the fall-of-load stage traced to two rules of the binary

Who: the developer (agent), unattended, with three investigations handed to copies of
itself working in trees of their own. Budget: about three hours of work, used as
`CLAUDE.md` says. Branch at the end: `dev/i7`.

## Goal

Record the owner's four answers of 2026-11-26. Measure r2x's time per case on the
development set first. Make r2c faster without changing a single result, with the test
that checks it in the first speed commit. Work on the stage that does not settle at the
fall of load when the web cracks. Planned: speed in the main line (it is measurable and
was known to be several times off), the stage at the fall of load in parallel.

## Done

- **The four answers**: `docs/brief.md` ("Owner's decisions since"), `CLAUDE.md`,
  `tools/heldout.py` (`KEPT`: priority 1 without shear stays at 99 %), `docs/measures.md`
  ("Speed").
- **r2x timed on all 164 development cases** (`tools/r2xtimes.py measure`,
  `tests/oracle/r2x-times.json`), six at a time, twice: reading the detail views of
  every stage, as the development captures were made (a median of 74 s without shear

  its default r2x itself fails on 8 of the 60 cases with shear ("Response has more than

- **The test that no record changes** (`tests/oracle/records.json`, `tools/same.py`,
  `tests/test_oracle.py`, `tests/test_speed.py`): the SHA-256 of r2c's whole record for

- **Speed, five commits, every record identical** (`docs/kernel.md`, "What is kept
  between evaluations, for speed"): the nodes' transverse solves kept by node
  (`NodeStore`; a node was solved eleven times over with the same inputs); the passes
  read the transverse stress alone; the nodes the last splitting asked for are solved
  together before the layers are split for the next state; the layers' nodes kept by
  their bounds; the stirrups' constants once a node; the splitting given only the four
  fields it reads. `tools/shadow.py` does every transverse solve of a case both ways
  and compares the bits; `tools/proftime.py` profiles a case.
- **Under shear alone r2c's own damping of the profile update is off** (`Shear.relax`),
  as trialled at the end of increment 6. Development set with shear: 55, 28, 51 of 60
  on priorities 1 to 3 and 36 of 58 on priority 4 (54, 27, 50, 35 before).
- **The stage at the fall of load under moment and shear, read** (newbeam-us-mv,
  Response's own tries against r2c's): the two programs agree to 0.02 % a pass up to
  the stage before the fall, r2c accepts that stage one pass before Response's profile
  error jumps, and after the change to the shear strain Response keeps an unsettled
  stage in the middle of the fall that r2c steps either side of. The stage control
  after the change of control variable is already Response's.
- **Where the 0.02 % a pass comes from: two rules, read from the binary**
  (`docs/kernel.md`, "The concrete's stiffness" and "The profile the first stage starts
  from"). The concrete's stiffness is the factored stress at 0.1 mm/m over the material
  factor plus one millionth (`004ac190`); the profile of the uncracked section is a
  table at stations a twenty-first of the depth apart, straight between (`004357d0`).
  With both, r2c's section stiffness on the first stages is Response's to six digits
  or more (the 1 % in the coupling terms that increment 5 did not find). **Neither is
  on**: see below.
- **Development cases outside** are as listed by `python tools/priority.py RUN`; no new
  case was added.

## Tried and dropped

- **The two rules of the binary, on.** The first alone: 162 development records change;
  the I-girder at M/V = 3 m leaves priority 1 (rising branch 23 %: the override takes
  over six stages earlier) and the I-girder with deeper strands leaves priority 3;
  nothing comes inside. Both together: priority 1 with shear 55 to 53, priority 2 28 to
  27, priority 3 51 to 50. On the I-girder at 3 m r2c follows 34 of Response's 58 tries
  throughout with the first rule and 35 without, so the rule is not what parts them.
  Left off because the held-out set is scored once and the development evidence was
  against: the first is in the kernel behind `materials.OWN_MODULUS`, the second on the
  branch `trial/i7-start-profile`. They are the first thing for the next increment
  (`docs/status.md`).
- **The first step after a try that ran away** (`Shear.stale_marks`, from Response's
  steps read in the running program, partly inferred): on the case it was read from
  the second try then fails at once as Response's does; on the 60 cases with shear
  priority 2 falls from 28 to 24. Written, off.
- **Speed, not exact, so not done**: evaluating only the layers still looked at when
  the layers are split (the nodes of a call stop closing in together, so a node's last
  digits depend on the nodes it is asked for with).
- **Speed, tried and no use**: the heights of the last two or three splittings in place
  of the last one (no faster on four cases); keeping a node's state by its strain
  rather than by the pass a call stops at (4 % of the states asked for come again).
  Estimated and not tried: solving further down under every layer that might split
  (the nodes cost as much as the small solves saved).
- **A hash of the record that included the input file's name.** The tests find the
  Quick Define inputs under r2x's name and `tools/verify.py` under the case's; five
  oracle tests failed on it. The hash now leaves that header line out and the file was
  rewritten; the run before any speed change is identical to the new hashes too.
- **Editing `src/` while a run was going.** A half-merged `shear.py` was read by the
  workers of a full run that started their first case in that minute: 22 cases failed
  with a syntax error and were run again. The same run's other 142 records were
  identical. Noted in `docs/status.md`.
