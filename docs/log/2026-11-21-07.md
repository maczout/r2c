## Result

Measured, sixth run against the fifth (step 3):

- inside on peak, rising branch and shape: 51 of 85 (43); of the 53 scope cases 27 (21);
- cases outside by check: peak 5 (11), rising 6 (7), shape 33 (42), post-peak 42 (44);
- shear responses that end as Response's does: 23 of 50 (14);
- **the 32 existing cases: no peak or rising check went outside**; two shape checks came
  inside (Marshall with shear, Newbeam with N₀ and M/V = 1 m: both now end standing still,
  as Response's does); no shape or post-peak check went outside;
- **dm-n1n: peak +0.10 % (it was +3.2 %)**, rising 0.08 %, shape the same, post-peak 3.8 %;
- Newbeam at M/V = 1 m, shear alone, and the no-stirrups case up to its peak are
  Response's stage for stage.

**Not reached.** The endings match in 23 of 50 shear cases, not in all. Five peaks are
still outside (softening 0, 5, 6, 8 on the I-girder; the T-beam), six rising branches, and
22 shear cases on the shape alone. They are recorded as known deviations in
`docs/status.md`. The rule is Response's now; what differs is whether one stage's sixteen
passes converge, and r2c's profile iteration converges at states where Response's does not
(and sometimes the other way round).

**C2 is not resolved.** Under axial load alone Response's control is the axial strain, and
on Marshall and Newbeam its response stays uniform to the end, as r2c's does (the two
agree to the last stage). Only the circular column leaves the uniform state. Its control
variable would change to the curvature only after five refused stages, and nothing in the
routines read refuses a uniform state past its peak on one section and not on another.

**G.** With shear: Response's end tests are r2c's. Without shear r2c keeps its own, which
come to the same on the cases compared (4·ε0 at the extreme fibre; the load run out; a
fifth of the peak after a rupture against Response's fifth of the peak from the eleventh
stage).

## Tried and dropped

- **Response's profile error measure on r2c's old profile grid**: errors of 2 to 4 % at
  converged stages, so most stages were refused and responses ended at a quarter of their
  peak. The cause was the grid, not the measure (above). Do not go back to r2c's own
  measure to get round it.
- **Damping the profile update under every loading** (half the update in the second half
  of the passes): shear alone then follows Response's falling branch, but r2c converges
  where Response's stages stand still and runs past its end (Marshall with shear +9.8 %,
  tension stiffening 6 +12.6 %). Kept for shear alone only.
- **Response's own relaxation rule as read** (damped by half once a stage has been
  refused, and from the start under axial strain control and for 0.15 h < M/V < 0.75 h):
  the same overshoots, and shear alone not fixed. Where that factor enters Response's
  solve was not read (it is not in the profile update); applying it to the profile update
  is not what Response does.
- **Stepping shear alone in the axial strain**, as the control-variable routine read for
  the beam class says: no state at the first step (the axial strain stays at nothing until
  cracking). Response's own stages under shear alone are evenly spaced in the shear
  strain, so that loading is taken to run in the other analysis class found (`0040e9f0`).
- **The collapsed last stage** where r2c's step dies on the plateau instead (Newbeam at
  M/V = 1 m: Response has two more stages, at 172 and 49 kN): they are unconverged states
  of Response's own iteration; r2c's attempts there fail in the global solve instead of
  coming back unconverged. Not forced.
- **C2 by refusing a uniform state past its peak** (state code 3, "every node beyond ε0"):
  would break Marshall and Newbeam under axial load alone, which Response keeps uniform.
