## Goal

## Done

- The cause was not the profile iteration. `tools/diagnose.py flow` (new) puts r2c in
  Response's own states and compares its calculated shear stress with the state's. Response's
  own two lines agree within 1 %; r2c's were 25 to 50 % off at Newbeam M/V = 2 m, stages 32
  to 42, with forces agreeing within 0.2 %.
- Kernel rule (inferred from output, `docs/kernel.md`): a bar on its yield plateau has the
  hardening-start slope in the shear flow calculation.
- The closest unconverged iterate is accepted within 3 %, not 5 %.
- Stepping (inferred from Response's control charts; the trigger is r2c's own): when no state
  is found at a larger curvature and the stirrups are past half of yield, the driver carries
  on in the reported shear strain. `Stage.cmode` records the control variable; More Detail
  adds stages only between stages found in the first one.
- `tests/test_oracle.py`: newbeam-m2v and newbeam-us-mv added to the slow set.
- Results regenerated; `docs/verification.md`, `docs/status.md`, `docs/kernel.md` updated.
- dm-n1n, first look: in `docs/verification.md`. No kernel change for it.

## Tried and dropped

All at Response's own states, measured with the scripts that became `diagnose.py flow`:

- Changing the profile iteration (damping, acceleration, a better start): not started. From
  Response's own profile and state r2c's iteration drifted away to a state with a tenth of
  the shear strain, because Response's state was not a fixed point of r2c's calculation. No
  iteration would have helped.
- Stirrups: the loading curve's stiffness instead of elastic unloading; no stiffness once
  yielded; the hardening slope on the plateau. Each moved the difference by under 1 point.
- Bars: elastic stiffness or secant stiffness when yielded: far worse (79 %).
- Bars: the larger of the average tangent and the tangent of the capacity at a crack: close
  on the Newbeam (2 to 4 %) but about 1.3 times the fitted stiffness; dropped for the
  hardening-start slope, which fits three steels.
- The whole shear flow by a finite difference of the section state, crack check included:
  12 to 18 %, and it puts shear flow in the cover concrete where Response has none. Response
  does not vary the flexural crack check in this calculation.
- For the difference that remains (stirrups yielded over much of the depth): solving the
  strain change along the member without a change of shear strain fits M/V = 0.5 m at stages
  37 to 39 (1 to 3 %) and nothing else (9 % before, 13 to 30 % after; 13 to 40 % at
  M/V = 2 m). Not a rule. Stopped here rather than fit further from two cases.
- The change to shear strain control whenever curvature stepping fails: Marshall changed over
  at stage 4, before cracking, and its peak moved by 1 % (onto a state between two of
  Response's curvature steps). Hence the condition on the stirrups.
- The same, only once stirrups have yielded: the US case never changes over (its stirrups
  are at 0.64 of yield at the last curvature stage).
