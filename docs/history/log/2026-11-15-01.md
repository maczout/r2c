## Goal

The owner's brief (2026-11-15): a Python clone of Response-2000's analytical core, matching r2x's
interface and capability so the two are interchangeable in a structural engineering workflow;
no features beyond what r2x covers; r2x's code may be reused; r2x is the oracle. Bentz's
thesis is the reference for the kernel's calculations; reverse engineering of the binary is
allowed to answer questions but not as the primary development approach.

## Done

- `r2c solve` with r2x's options, exit statuses and record format.
- The kernel: MCFT node with crack check, dynamic layering, flexure and axial driver, shear by
  the longitudinal stiffness method (numpy only).
- `tools/compare.py`, `tools/verify.py`, `tools/trim.py`; 25 oracle fixtures; pytest tests.
- `docs/verification.md` and `docs/kernel.md`.

## Tried and dropped

- Shear profile iteration with fixed damping, past the first yielding of the stirrups (Newbeam
  at M/V = 2 m and similar): it diverged or settled on a different state (V about 4 % higher,
  much smaller shear strain). Started from Response's state just before the dip, r2c
  reproduced Response's later stages within 0.2 % in V: the kernel can represent those states;
  the iteration cannot reach them.

## Decisions

- No pass or fail threshold, by agreement (owner and developer). Now superseded by

  Response stage for stage (each marked in `docs/kernel.md`). Whether this extent was within
  the brief was left to the owner (see the 2026-11-17 log).
